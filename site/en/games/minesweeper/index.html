Goal

Open every cell without a mine. A number gives the total of the mines around its cell.

No luck

  • Your first move is always safe: the grid is only created after it, and it opens an area.
  • A grid is only offered if a solver finished it entirely by deduction, seeing only what you see: the numbers and the total number of mines. You never need to guess.
  • Some deductions are hard, especially in dense variants. If no grid is found in time, the game says so rather than offering a doubtful one: lower the number of mines.

Controls

  • Mouse: left click to open, right click for a flag or a question mark. A click on a number, or both buttons together, opens its neighbours if it has enough flags around it.
  • Touch screen: tap to open, long press to mark, tap a number to open its neighbours.
  • Keyboard and mouse: on the hovered cell, Enter opens and Space marks, or opens a number's neighbours.
  • Shortcuts: F2 for a new game, Esc to close a window, + and − to zoom, H for a hint.
  • Keyboard only: arrow keys to move, Enter to open, F or Space to mark.
  • With several mine types, marking again changes the flag's type.
  • "Swap buttons", in the options, exchanges the two actions, with a mouse or a finger.
  • The hint shows a safe cell using only what you can see. A game with a hint does not count in the statistics.
  • Your game is saved: you find it again when you come back to the page.

Surfaces

  • Cylinder: the left and right edges touch.
  • Torus: the top and bottom touch as well.
  • Möbius strip: left joins right upside down.
  • Klein bottle: Möbius, and the top touches the bottom.

The numbers on the edges show the joins: two equal numbers mark the same row or column.

What a number counts

  • Neighbours count double: cells sharing a side are worth 2, the others 1.
  • Knight's move: the 8 cells a chess knight's move away.
  • Cross: the 4 cells sharing a side.
  • Star: the 8 neighbours, plus the 4 cells two steps away in a straight line.
  • Far ring: only the square of cells two steps away (or, with hexagons, the second ring).
  • Looking up: the 6 cells of the two rows above, on a torus only.
  • Two rings: with hexagons, the 18 closest cells.

Mines

A double mine counts for 2 and a triple for 3. An anti-mine counts for −1: a 0 can hide mines that cancel out, and only a truly mine-free area opens by itself.

Colour mode

Each cell gives two pieces of information: the number counts the mines on the cross, and the colour counts those on the diagonals. With hexagons, each counts every other direction. The dots under the number repeat the colour. This mode uses single mines and normal neighbours.

Where the idea comes from

These variants are inspired by the Minesveeper advent calendar by Heptaveegesimal. The sizes follow those of démineur.eu.
